Case study 01

Concurrency-safe deposits in a multi-party booking workflow

Preventing duplicate bookings and inconsistent payment outcomes while replacing a mature operations product.

  • Elixir
  • Phoenix LiveView
  • PostgreSQL
  • Ecto
  • Stripe
  • Oban

Context and challenge

A mature Rails back-office product was being replaced with a Phoenix/LiveView application for direct collaboration between service buyers and suppliers. Deposit-backed bookings introduced retries, browser/webhook concurrency, changing proposal availability, and the risk of payment diverging from booking confirmation.

My contribution

I substantially contributed to the proposal and booking domains, authorization, LiveView workflows, tests, and validation-first tenant migration tooling. For deposit-backed bookings, I implemented the pending state, database uniqueness and duplicate recovery, amount-safe draft reuse, row locking, atomic local transitions, and explicit stale or conflict outcomes.

Result

Duplicate requests could not create multiple pending bookings. Competing completion paths serialized against the same booking, and abandoned attempts could resume without charging an outdated amount.

Read implementation perspective

The workflow represented waiting for a deposit explicitly instead of confirming prematurely. Within the wider team implementation, browser and webhook completion converged on one finalization workflow, avoiding separate business rules for synchronous and asynchronous completion.

The broader replacement gave buyers and suppliers a simpler shared workflow while preserving continuity for selected tenants migrated from the previous application.

Engineering perspective: model intermediate states explicitly, enforce invariants in PostgreSQL, use locks where concurrent actors converge, keep local transitions atomic, and make exceptional outcomes visible.

Case study 02

Improving financial consistency in a Stripe payment system

Keeping participant-level balances and local charge state correct across retries and asynchronous updates.

  • Elixir
  • Phoenix
  • PostgreSQL
  • Ecto
  • Stripe Connect
  • Auditing

Context and challenge

A multi-tenant SaaS platform collected configurable installment payments. A single payment could cover multiple participants with separate balances, packages, fees, discounts, and deposit requirements. Aggregate validation could hide an individual overpayment, while request and webhook paths could race to update one charge.

My contribution

I implemented or substantially modified local charge creation with participant-level lines, explicit statuses and allowed transitions, row locking for request/webhook races, participant-level overpayment and deposit validation, and audited financial writes.

Result

Fewer balance and overpayment problems, fewer race-condition-related production errors, easier investigation and reconciliation, and better traceability for support staff.

Read implementation perspective

The wider team implementation also used application and provider idempotency, timeout recovery, asynchronous webhook handling, and method-specific payment states. My work ensured those paths converged on guarded, auditable local financial records.

Engineering perspective: payment reliability requires precise local accounting, explicit state transitions, concurrency control, auditability, and validation at the level where funds are allocated.

Case study 03

Centralizing identity across multiple SaaS applications

Replacing duplicated per-product onboarding with one secure, auditable administrative control plane.

  • Elixir
  • Phoenix LiveView
  • PostgreSQL
  • Oban
  • TOTP
  • HMAC
  • Terraform

Context and challenge

Tenant and user onboarding happened separately inside multiple SaaS products. The new control plane needed multi-tenant membership, role-aware product access, secure support, auditable impersonation, privileged authentication, and reliable synchronization with downstream applications.

My contribution

I worked across identity and membership modeling, invitations, scoped authorization, audited impersonation, TOTP recovery, LiveView administration, signed downstream webhooks, Oban retries and stale-update protection, Terraform previews, and security-focused CI.

Result

Onboarding and access administration moved into one application, noticeably simplifying the process while establishing explicit identity, tenant, and product-access boundaries.

Read implementation perspective

Asynchronous synchronization allowed administrative writes to succeed even when downstream products were unavailable. Queued updates could detect and cancel stale delivery attempts before overwriting newer state.

Engineering perspective: centralized identity requires more than a shared users table—it needs separated actor identities, explicit authorization scopes, auditable privileged access, secure event delivery, and a strategy for eventual consistency.
